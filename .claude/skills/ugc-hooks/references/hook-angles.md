# Hook angles — worked examples

Generic placeholder product: a focus/energy supplement. Replace with the real
offer and persona voice. Each example is ≤12 words and works as an *opener*.

### 1. Problem / pain call-out
- "If you crash every afternoon, this is for you."
- "Coffee jitters ruining your mornings? Same."

### 2. Curiosity / pattern interrupt
- "Nobody tells you why energy drinks stop working."
- "I found the thing labels don't want you reading."

### 3. Bold claim / result
- "This replaced my third cup of coffee completely."
- "Two weeks in and my 3pm crash is gone."

### 4. Myth-bust / contrarian
- "More caffeine is not the answer. Here's why."
- "Energy 'boosters' are mostly sugar. This isn't."

### 5. Us vs them / comparison
- "Coffee vs this, side by side, for 30 days."
- "I tested the viral one against the cheap one."

### 6. Social proof / trend
- "Why is everyone's desk suddenly stocked with these?"
- "My whole office switched to this in a week."

### 7. Skeptic-to-convert
- "I rolled my eyes at this. I was wrong."
- "Did NOT expect a supplement to actually do this."

### 8. Question hook
- "Why does no one talk about afternoon crashes?"
- "What if the jitters weren't part of the deal?"

### 9. Demonstration tease
- "Watch how fast this dissolves — no chalky mess."
- "Let me show you what's actually in this."

### 10. Authority / credentials
- "I've tried 40 of these. This is the one."
- "As someone who reads supplement labels for fun…"

## Notes
- The on-screen caption can be punchier/shorter than the spoken hook.
- When in doubt, lead with the *viewer's* problem, not the product.
- Keep the persona's verbal tics consistent across angles.
